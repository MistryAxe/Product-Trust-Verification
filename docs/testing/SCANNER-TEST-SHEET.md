# Scanner test sheet: how to collect real results so the scanner can be tuned

**Why:** the scanner depends on what real public databases and websites know about real products. I cannot reach them from where I work,
so I need your real results. Thirty minutes of scanning gives me more than days of guessing.

## 1. Choose 12 to 15 products you can hold in your hand
Mix them on purpose:
- 4 supermarket **food and drink** items (include bread, Sprite, a South African brand, something local like maize meal)
- 3 **household** items (the Airoma lavender freshener, a cleaner, a washing powder)
- 2 **beauty / toiletry** items
- 2 **electronics** (the Radisson fish lamp, a phone charger)
- 2 **obscure or local** items you suspect no database knows (a small local brand, a market product)
- 1 product you **registered through the manufacturer account** in the app (this is your control: it must always be right)

## 2. For each product (3 minutes)
1. Sign in as a **customer** and scan it. Close the app completely between products (a "not found" is remembered until the app restarts).
2. Take a **screenshot of the first result screen**.
3. If a product page opens, tap the **Sources** tab and take a screenshot. This is the most useful picture: it shows which databases and
   websites the app used.
4. If the app says **"We could not identify this product"**, tap **Photograph the Label**, photograph the front of the pack, and note
   whether it then found the right product.
5. Take one clear photo of the real pack (front, and the barcode).
6. Write one line in the table below.

**Barcode numbers:** you do not need to type them. Open **Home > History**: every scan shows its barcode number and the name the app found.

## 3. The table (copy this into a message or a document)
| # | What the product really is | Barcode digits | What the app showed (name / brand) | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sprite 500 ml | 5449000... | ... | Right / Wrong / Could not identify | e.g. found after label photo |

**Verdict words:**
- **Right**: the name and brand match the real pack.
- **Wrong**: it showed a different product (the serious one, so always send the Sources screenshot).
- **Could not identify**: honest, acceptable. Say whether the label photo fixed it.
- **Partly**: right product but important details missing.

## 4. What to send me
In one chat message: the table, then the screenshots grouped by product number (you can attach about ten images at a time, so send in
batches and say "batch 1 of 2"). Say which phone and whether you were on Wi-Fi or mobile data.

## 5. What I will do with it
I look for patterns: which sources answered, which products no source knew, which searches return junk. Then I adjust what the scanner
asks, how it checks pages, and which wording the app shows. Some South African products will simply not exist in any free source;
the honest "could not identify" plus the label photo is the correct answer for those. The goal is **never a wrong product**, and as
few "could not identify" results as the real world allows.
